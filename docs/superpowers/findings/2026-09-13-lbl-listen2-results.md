## 2. What I infer from it — in plain language

**Labelled as inference. The measurements are §1; disagree with this section freely.**

1. **Accepting a connection two listeners support, instead of four, did not change what he
   heard.** He judged 24 side-by-side comparisons without knowing which side was which, on two
   questions, and came out two rows apart on each against a bar of eight. That is the whole
   finding, and it is the question listen 2 was built to ask.

2. **A large part of why he could not tell them apart is that on many rows there was nothing
   to tell apart.** Five of the 24 comparisons put the *same journey* on both sides — he said
   so, unprompted, in those words. On several more, his note is that the artists he did not
   know were the *same artists* on both sides. Pairs were chosen so the two maps disagree; they
   were not, and could not be, chosen so the two maps disagree **in artists he has never heard
   of**, and that second thing is what the novelty question actually needs.

3. **The small difference there was points the way the theory says it should.** The looser bar
   gave him slightly more new artists and slightly less coherent journeys. Two rows each way
   out of 24 is the kind of gap an instrument this coarse produces by accident, so **I would
   not act on the direction** — but it is the trade the design anticipated rather than a
   random-looking pair of zeros.

4. **The fix list from listen 1 solved the problems it named and did not buy resolution.**
   Longer journeys: delivered, every cell had at least three artists in the middle. Pairs where
   the maps genuinely differ: delivered. Balanced sides, three clips, strength on the row: all
   delivered and all working. And he marked *no preference* on **30 of 48 answers**, against 26
   of 48 last time. **The rows he cannot call are not short rows or identical-map rows — they
   are rows where he already knows everyone who differs.** That is a different problem from the
   one listen 1 diagnosed, and it is a property of the *listener*, not of the pairs or the
   harness.

5. **Asking him directly whether he was trading one thing off against the other produced
   nothing.** Twenty-four "no"s, including on the single row where he picked one side for
   coherence and the other for novelty. Listen 1 got that information three times from his free
   text *without* asking for it. Either he does not experience it as a trade, or the question as
   worded does not reach the thing his notes describe.

6. **Asking how strong each pick was earned its place.** It is the only instrument in the listen
   that can say the incumbent's coherence lead is seven hairlines, and that the one time he used
   the word "definitely" he was describing the challenger. **It decides nothing and must not** —
   but a reader who saw only "7 versus 5" would take away something the listener did not say.

## 3. Weakest link

**The load-bearing assumption is that 24 rows at a bar of 8 can detect a difference the owner's
ear would care about.** It is weaker here than in listen 1, for a reason that is measured rather
than suspected: **five rows could not carry a pick at all**, so the bar of 8 was applied to a
live denominator of 19. Three-quarters of the novelty rows and half the coherence rows returned
*no preference*, and his notes attribute most of them to already knowing everyone who differed.

**What would falsify it:** a listen whose pairs are screened on **differential novelty** — the
two maps differing specifically in artists outside his familiarity export — returning a margin.
If that moved past the bar, this tie was a resolution limit rather than a fact about the two
thresholds. Nothing here says such a screen is worth running; that is §6's territory and the
owner's.

**What I would defend:** the tally, the run state, and that `LBL-R2` is correctly applied.
`lbl_unblind.py`'s `axis_read` and `listen_read` were checked clause by clause against
`LBD-AM5-5` — the bar of 8, *no preference* counting for neither, the underpowered branch
requiring margin < 8 **and** ≥ 8 clip-blocked no-preference rows, a margin ≥ 8 standing however
many rows were lost, a split resolving to FAIL, and strength never reaching a verdict — and
`MARGIN`, `DEPTHS`, `AXES`, `MIN_INTERIOR_BY_LISTEN[2] = 3`, `SIDE_ASSIGNMENT_BY_LISTEN[2] =
dealt_balanced` and `ROW_EXTRAS_BY_LISTEN[2]` were checked in `lbl_common.py`. All correct as
written. The tally was independently recomputed by hand before the result file was opened.

**What I would abandon cheaply:** the direction of either margin, and any claim that the
challenger buys novelty at a cost in coherence. That rests on two rows per axis.

**What I cannot bound at all:** whether the five identical cells are a property of these pairs
or of the two maps generally. The pre-screen's own figures would say, and they are map-labelled;
this note does not read them.

## 4. Instrument defects this run exposed, for any future listen

Recorded so that a future protocol has them; **no session proposes a future listen** — that is
§6's territory.

1. **Gate 2 lets a pair through with one dead depth.** Requiring difference at 2 of 3 depths
   permitted five identical cells, four of them at d0 — the depth `LBD-AM5-5` deliberately put
   in the tally. A future screen should require difference at **every** depth, or the bar should
   be set on live cells rather than on nominal ones. Either is a pre-registration change, not a
   reading choice.
2. **Interior-set difference is not novelty difference.** The screen guaranteed the maps differ;
   the novelty question needs them to differ in artists the listener does not know. The gap is
   measurable in advance from the same familiarity export the ranking already uses.
3. **The familiarity export under-reads what he knows, or the difference sits inside it.** Pairs
   selected as having one or two familiar interiors out of thirteen to twenty-five still drew
   *"no artist was unfamiliar here"*. Whichever it is, the export cannot currently be trusted as
   a proxy for what a row will feel like.
4. **`LBL-Q3` returned no signal.** Twenty-four "no"s including the opposite-picks row. Whether
   a row traded one axis against the other is **computable from the row itself** — it is exactly
   the case where the two answers name different sides — so asking it spends the listener's
   attention on something the data already carries.
5. **`LBL-Q4` worked and should be carried forward, with its treatment unchanged.** Asking on
   the row, in advance, with the tally blind to it, is what made it admissible (`LBD-AM6-5`), and
   it is the only thing separating a landslide row from a hairline one.
6. **Three clips per artist held for the second listen running.** Zero rows lost. Carry forward.
7. **The 4–4 deal did its job.** An 11–7 right lean became role-neutral by construction rather
   than by luck. Carry forward.
8. **Clip coverage is not complete and the gap is silent.** One artist (Goo Goo Dolls) had no
   clip; it cost nothing only because he appeared in the same position on both sides. A
   differential gap would have cost the row without anyone noticing it was a resolver problem
   rather than a map problem.
